## 核心論點

攻擊端 AI 已拿到作弊器——成本三千美元，品質世界頂尖。防禦端卻仍依賴季度預算週期。這不是技術差距，是決策週期的結構性失速。

---

## 一、攻擊者只花了三千美元

想像一個場景：某個中型企業的 IT 主管，剛完成年度滲透測試，報告顯示「高風險漏洞 2 個，已排入下季修補計畫」。他對這份報告感到滿意。

同一天，世界另一個角落，有人用不到 $3,000 美元，讓 AI 自動生成了一個針對類似系統的遠端代碼執行（RCE）漏洞利用程式——品質達到全球頂尖滲透測試工程師的水準。

這不是假設。這是 AI Ascent 2026 峰會上，XBOW 創辦人展示的實際案例。

**XBOW 的 AI 已在 HackerOne 全球漏洞賞金平台奪得第一名。**

不是「進入前十」。是第一。

---

## 二、攻防比例的結構性崩潰

資安行業有一條長期成立的非對稱定律：攻擊者只需要找到一個缺口，防禦者需要封住每一個缺口。這條定律一直都在，但 AI 讓它的不對稱比例急劇惡化。

### CVE 延遲已轉負

傳統上，漏洞從被發現到被修補，中間存在一個「修補窗口」——這個窗口雖然令人不安，但至少存在。

XBOW 數據顯示，**CVE 的「修補延遲」已轉為負值**。

這意味著：攻擊端 AI 發現並利用漏洞的速度，已超越防禦端發布修補程式的速度。修補窗口不再是窗口，它已變成攻擊者的領先優勢。

### 六到九個月的倒數計時

更令人警覺的是 XBOW 對開源模型的估計：**六到九個月後，開源 AI 模型將達到與 XBOW 相同等級的自主攻擊能力。**

目前 XBOW 的技術尚需商業模型支撐，但六到九個月後，任何能存取開源模型的人，都可能擁有等效能力。攻擊門檻將在短期內崩潰。

---

## 三、「安全假設」的時代已結束

過去二十年的企業資安邏輯，建立在幾個隱性前提上：

- 攻擊者是人，速度有限
- 滲透測試覆蓋的範圍代表真實風險面
- 有時間窗口修補已知漏洞
- 防禦的成本效益比攻擊更划算

AI 攻擊工具正在逐一摧毀這些假設。XBOW 創辦人的結語是一個警告，也是一個現實確認：每一個組織，**現在**就需要假設對手擁有 AI 等級的滲透能力。不是「可能」，不是「未來某天」，是現在。

---

## 四、Zero-Trust 從選項變為必須

「Zero-Trust」在資安圈已講了十年，很多組織把它當作中長期架構目標，排在路線圖的第三年或第五年。AI 攻擊能力的躍升，讓「第三年」變成了「昨天就該完成」。

Zero-Trust 的核心邏輯恰好對抗 AI 攻擊的兩大優勢：

**對抗速度優勢**：Zero-Trust 不依賴「發現攻擊後再響應」，而是預設信任從不存在，每次存取都需驗證。AI 再快，沒有橫向移動空間，速度優勢大幅降低。

**對抗規模優勢**：AI 可同時掃描數千個端點，但 Zero-Trust 的微分段（Micro-segmentation）讓每個端點都是獨立戰場，即使突破一點，也無法串聯成規模性損害。

---

## 五、給決策者的三道思考題

這場演講之後，有三個問題值得每一位企業決策者認真回答。

**你的攻擊面，是以人類駭客還是 AI 為基準評估的？** 兩者之間的差距，不是倍數，是量級。如果你的滲透測試仍以人類工程師的能力為假設基礎，那份報告呈現的風險，已是系統性低估。

**你的修補週期，還撐得住負值窗口嗎？** 季度修補的邏輯，建立在「漏洞發布後有時間緩衝」的前提上。這個前提，在 CVE 延遲轉負的現實下已不成立。

**資安預算對話的邏輯，需要翻轉嗎？** 更準確的問法是：「不投資的機會成本，是否已超過投資本身？」在 AI 攻擊能力的新現實下，答案幾乎是肯定的。

## 結語

AI Ascent 2026 有很多令人興奮的前景：AGI 進度、具身機器人、生命科學革命。但對於負責守護組織資產的人而言，XBOW 那場演講傳遞的訊息更為緊迫：

**攻擊端已拿到作弊器。防禦端能做的，是讓這張牌盡可能難以打出。**

而 Zero-Trust，是目前最接近這個目標的架構選擇。

## Core Argument

Attack-side AI now has cheat codes — $3,000 buys world-class capability. Defense still runs on quarterly budget cycles. This is not a technology gap. It is a structural lag in decision-making cadence.

---

## I. The Attacker Spent $3,000

Imagine this: an IT manager at a mid-sized enterprise has just completed the annual penetration test. The report shows two high-risk vulnerabilities, both scheduled for remediation next quarter. He's satisfied with the result.

That same day, somewhere else in the world, someone uses less than $3,000 to let an AI autonomously generate a Remote Code Execution (RCE) exploit targeting a similar system — at a quality level matching the world's top penetration testing engineers.

This isn't hypothetical. It's what XBOW's founder demonstrated at AI Ascent 2026.

**XBOW's AI ranked #1 on HackerOne, the world's largest bug bounty platform.**

Not "top ten." Number one.

---

## II. The Structural Collapse of Attack-Defense Ratios

Cybersecurity has long operated under an asymmetric rule: attackers need to breach only one defense; defenders must hold every single line. AI has dramatically worsened the asymmetry ratio.

### CVE Remediation Delay Has Gone Negative

Traditionally, a "patching window" exists between vulnerability discovery and remediation — uncomfortable, but at least present.

XBOW's data shows that **CVE remediation delay has turned negative**.

This means: the speed at which attack-side AI discovers and exploits vulnerabilities now exceeds the speed at which defenders release patches. The patching window is no longer a window — it has become a head start for attackers.

### A 6–9 Month Countdown

XBOW's estimate for open-source models: **within 6 to 9 months, open-source AI models will reach the same level of autonomous attack capability as XBOW**.

What today requires commercial models will, within half a year, be replicable by anyone with access to open-source tools. The barrier to sophisticated cyberattacks is about to collapse.

---

## III. The Era of "Security Assumptions" Is Over

Two decades of enterprise security logic rested on implicit premises:

- Attackers are human, with human-speed limits
- Penetration test coverage represents real risk exposure
- There is time to patch known vulnerabilities
- Defense has better cost-efficiency than offense

AI attack tools are dismantling these assumptions one by one. XBOW's founder closed with a quiet and weighty observation: every organization needs to assume — **right now** — that adversaries have AI-grade penetration capabilities. Not "possibly." Not "someday." Now.

---

## IV. Zero-Trust: From Option to Requirement

"Zero-Trust" has circulated in enterprise IT for over a decade. Many organizations have placed it in year three or year five of the roadmap. The leap in AI attack capability turns "year three" into "should have been done yesterday."

Zero-Trust's core logic directly counters AI-driven attacks on two fronts:

**Against speed advantage**: Zero-Trust assumes trust never exists — every access request requires re-verification, every node is an independent boundary. Without lateral movement space, speed advantage has nowhere to go.

**Against scale advantage**: AI can scan thousands of endpoints simultaneously, but Zero-Trust's micro-segmentation makes each endpoint an independent battlefield. Even if one point is breached, the damage cannot cascade into systemic collapse.

---

## V. Three Questions for Decision-Makers

**Is your attack surface assessed against human hackers or AI?** The gap between the two is not a multiplier — it is a difference in magnitude. If your penetration testing still assumes human engineer capabilities as the baseline, that report is a systematic underestimate.

**Can your patching cycle survive a negative remediation window?** Quarterly patching logic was built on the premise that time exists after a vulnerability is disclosed. That premise no longer holds.

**Does the security budget conversation need to be reframed?** The more accurate question now is: "Does the cost of not investing already exceed the cost of investing?" Under the new reality of AI attack capabilities, the answer is almost certainly yes.

---

## Closing

AI Ascent 2026 offered many inspiring visions: AGI progress, embodied robotics, life sciences revolution. But for those responsible for protecting organizational assets, the message from the XBOW session was more urgent:

**The attacker has cheat codes. What defense can do is make those cards as hard as possible to play.**

And Zero-Trust is currently the architecture choice closest to achieving that goal.
