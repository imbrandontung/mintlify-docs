# English Version

We've covered the moment Fable 5 went public, how the free oversight tool still makes money, and the strategic logic behind giving it away. This post is the operational answer: what should integrators, individuals, and the next decade actually do?

## Three operating principles for a world where both sides have AI

### ① Defense moves first

The same capability is available to both attackers and defenders. The honest framing is: use it on the defense side, faster and better than the other side does.

Restricting access doesn't stop the capability from spreading — it just buys time. Use the time well.

### ② Finding holes is easy. Fixing them is hard.

Finding vulnerabilities just became industrialized. Fixing them, prioritizing them, and coordinating the patches did not.

Value permanently shifts from "find more problems" to "fix problems faster." The long-term opportunity is on the fixing side, not the finding side.

### ③ Oversight is the differentiator, not the cost

Trustworthy, auditable, boundary-respecting oversight isn't a compliance tax. It's the thing customers will pay extra for.

Security leaders have a professional duty that requires it, regardless of whether sales people position it nicely.

## For integration companies

**Sell capability and speed — not bodies.** AI compressed the time-to-fix window to almost nothing. The loop of "find → prioritize → patch" can only be run by the customer themselves; you can't insert manual labor into that loop fast enough. Your role is enabler: package the products, the automation, and the integration that lets the customer run that loop on their own at the speed it now requires.

**Stand on the trusted-defender side.** Position yourself as the customer's AI security partner — turning AI on, watching how it's used, proving to auditors what happened. Three things, one role.

**Build a layered offering across the AI workflow.** Think of "AI inside a company" as four layers — and attach a product to each:

| Layer | What it does | Examples of tools |
|---|---|---|
| **Data going in** | Prevent sensitive data from being typed into AI in the first place | Data-loss prevention, data masking, data security posture |
| **Who can access** | Decide who in the company is allowed to use AI, and at what level | Identity & permissions + turning on the free oversight hook |
| **What the AI is doing** | Watch AI agents for unsafe actions | Prompt-injection defense, agent behavior monitoring, red-line policy |
| **Audit and review** | Route the logs into the company's existing security dashboards | SIEM routing, shadow AI detection |

For a security leader, this is a layered, purchasable value map — not an abstract slogan.

## For individual users

**Assume both sides have AI.** Phishing and exploitation are now faster and more convincing. Multi-factor authentication, password managers, immediate software updates, least-privilege access — these stopped being "recommended" and started being "required."

**Use the defender's bonus.** The same AI that helps attackers also helps you check suspicious messages, audit your own digital footprint, and understand your privacy settings. The bonus is available to you too. Use it.

**Practice data hygiene with the AI tools you use.** Anything you type into an AI may be logged and audited later. Mask sensitive data before pasting. Understand where your tool's data goes.

## Four directions for security people thinking about the next decade

1. **Fix faster, not find more.** Shift budget from "buy more detection tools" to "shorten the time from finding a hole to closing it." Real-time software inventory, automated patching, temporary defenses while waiting for patches.
2. **Treat AI oversight as a standard capability.** Wire up the free oversight hook, audit AI usage, set red-line policies, detect employees using AI outside official channels. Package this as something you can deliver to customers as a repeatable module.
3. **Design assuming the capability will spread.** Don't bet on restrictions lasting forever. Build with defense in depth, minimize the blast radius when something goes wrong, and make patches deployable fast.
4. **Train people to be AI-literate about security.** Teach employees and ordinary users to spot AI-generated phishing, use AI to protect themselves, and understand what they're feeding their tools.

## Closing the series

Fable 5 closed the "limited access" framing yesterday. The question is no longer who gets the capability — it's how everyone manages the capability they already have.

Thanks for reading all four. Pushback, corrections, and counter-examples welcome.

# 中文版

前三篇講了 Fable 5 公開上線的時刻、免費工具怎麼賺錢、為什麼免費送的策略邏輯。這篇是「實作答案」：整合商、個人、未來十年，到底該怎麼做？

## 「攻防皆 AI 化」世界的三條操作原則

### ① 防禦先動

同樣的能力，攻擊方和防禦方都拿得到。誠實的框架是：用在防禦側，比對手用得更快、更好。

限制存取阻止不了能力擴散——只爭取到時間。把時間用好。

### ② 找漏洞容易、修補很難

漏洞發現剛剛被工業化。修補、優先級、修補編排沒有。

價值永久從「找出更多問題」推向「更快修好問題」。長期機會在修補側，不在發現側。

### ③ 監看是差異化，不是成本

可信、可稽核、尊重邊界的監看不是合規稅。它是客戶會額外付費的那一塊。

資安主管的專業責任本來就要求他們做這件事，跟銷售包裝無關。

## 對整合公司

**賣「能力」與「速度」，不賣人頭。** AI 把修補時間壓到極短。「發現 → 優先級 → 修補」這個閉環只能由客戶自己跑，你來不及把人工塞進去。你的角色是賦能者：把產品、自動化、整合包好，讓客戶自己用 AI 該有的速度跑這個閉環。

**站在「可信防禦者」一側。** 把自己定位成客戶的 AI 資安夥伴——把 AI 打開來用、看員工怎麼用、向稽核證明發生了什麼。三件事，一個角色。

**沿著 AI 工作流分層搭產品。** 把「公司內的 AI」想成四層，每層搭一個產品：

| 層級 | 做什麼 | 對應工具 |
|---|---|---|
| **資料進去之前** | 防止敏感資料被打進 AI | 資料外洩防護、資料遮罩、資料安全姿態 |
| **誰可以用** | 決定公司裡誰能用 AI、能用到什麼等級 | 身分與權限 + 打開免費監看鉤子 |
| **AI 在做什麼** | 看著 AI 代理人有沒有做不安全的事 | 擋住 prompt 注入攻擊、Agent 行為監控、紅線政策 |
| **稽核與覆盤** | 把紀錄導進公司現有的資安儀表板 | SIEM 路由、影子 AI 偵測 |

對資安主管，這是「分層可採購的價值地圖」，不是抽象口號。

## 對個人使用者

**假設攻防都已 AI 化。** 釣魚和漏洞利用更快、更逼真。雙因子驗證、密碼管理器、即時更新、最小權限——從「建議」升級為「必需」。

**善用防禦方紅利。** 同樣讓攻擊方更強的 AI，也讓你檢查可疑訊息、稽核自己的數位足跡、理解隱私設定。紅利你也拿得到。拿去用。

**對你用的 AI 工具保持資料衛生。** 打進 AI 的東西可能被記錄、被稽核。敏感資料先遮罩再貼。理解工具的資料會流到哪裡去。

## 給資安從業人員的四個方向（思考未來十年）

1. **修得更快，不是找得更多。** 預算從「買更多偵測工具」轉向「縮短發現到修好的時間」。即時的軟體清單、自動化修補、等正式修補前的暫時防禦。
2. **把 AI 監看當標準能力。** 把免費監看鉤子接好、稽核 AI 用量、設定紅線政策、偵測影子 AI。封裝成可以重複交付給客戶的模組。
3. **設計時假設能力會擴散。** 別賭限制能維持很久。縱深防禦、把出事時的爆炸半徑壓到最小、讓修補可以快速上線。
4. **訓練人變得「對 AI 有資安識讀」。** 教員工和一般使用者辨識 AI 釣魚、用 AI 保護自己、理解他們餵給工具的東西。

## 連載收尾

Fable 5 昨天關上了「限制存取」這個框架。問題不再是「誰拿到能力」——而是「每個人怎麼管理他們已經拿到的能力」。
